# Articles and clippings

This source is a piece someone else published: news, a blog post, an essay, documentation
the user saved. It belongs to its author.

- Most articles need no page of their own. Put what they say on the subject pages it
  concerns, attributed to the author or outlet with the date: "…에 따르면(2026-03)".
- Give the piece its own page only when it is a substantial argument worth returning to,
  or the user commented on it at length: its thesis, the main reasons, and the user's
  response in a separate section.
- Tell reporting from opinion, and one outlet's claim from an established fact. News is
  bound to its date: say when it was written and write time-sensitive statements as of
  that date.
- The user's comments on the piece are the core and stay distinct from the author's words.
- Paraphrase. Quote at most a sentence or two, and only when the wording itself matters.
- Assign navigation, advertisements, related-link lists, and comment sections to `null`.
- When you supplement a claim that matters, check it against a reference you read rather
  than repeating the article.
