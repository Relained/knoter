# Books and reading notes

This source is the user's notes on a book: chapter summaries, quotations, reactions.

- One page for the book, titled with its title, with the author (and translator or
  edition when given) in the first lines. Cover what the notes cover: the book's point in
  a sentence or two, its parts or chapters, passages the user marked, and the user's own
  thoughts and questions in their own section.
- Later notes on the same book extend that page with `sections` edits, chapter by chapter;
  do not start a second page. Say how far the notes reach when the reading is partial.
- An idea that outlives the book goes to its concept page, attributed to the author and
  linked to the book's page. For fiction, keep characters and themes as sections of the
  book's page unless the user follows them across books.
- Keep marked passages short and exact, with the page number when the notes give one.
  Everything else is paraphrased; the book's text belongs to its author.
- The user's reactions, disagreements, and what they mean to do with the book are the
  core. Do not replace them with a review.
- Supplements, marked as always: who the author is, when and why the book was written,
  how it was received, and what the notes leave out. Do not invent plot or arguments the
  notes do not mention without marking them as a supplement backed by a reference.
- `category` is the book's subject; tag with `책` and its themes.
