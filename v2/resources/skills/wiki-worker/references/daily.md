# Dated notes that mix everything

This note is named for a day, and the user writes whatever came up that day into it:
a lecture, a stray idea, something that happened, a to-do, a pasted passage. It is not a
diary and not one subject. Treat every paragraph or heading block as its own part.

- Go through the segments in order and decide the kind of each one on its own: study
  note, journal, paper, article, book, project record, or fragment. Neighbouring segments
  join into one part only when they continue the same subject; a new subject starts a new
  part even without a heading.
- Give every part its own entry in `topics`, pointing at the page that subject belongs
  to. Two paragraphs about different things never share a page because they share a day.
- No page is named after the date, and no page collects "what the note said". The date is
  context: mention it where it matters (when something was learned, decided, or happened).
- A part about the user's own day (what happened, how they felt, who they met) goes to
  the month's page `일기/YYYY-MM` as a section headed with the date, in their voice,
  without interpretation or advice and without supplements. Never put private names or
  details from it into a search or reference request.
- A to-do or plan goes to the page of the project or subject it concerns as its state on
  that date; a stray idea or question becomes a short, tentative section on the page it
  concerns.
- Supplements follow each part's kind: a study part gets them as usual, a personal part
  gets none.
- One-line scratch (a reminder with no content, a bare link, a repeated line) goes to
  `null` with a reason rather than onto a page.
