# Papers

This source is a research paper, or the user's notes on one. It is one work with one
argument, so it keeps a page of its own in addition to what it adds to subject pages.

- The paper's page is titled with the paper's title (shortened when long); put the
  authors and year, the venue or "preprint" when stated, and the DOI or arXiv address in
  the first lines, and declare the common short name and `저자 연도` as `terms`. Cover, as
  far as the source does: the question, the claim in one or two sentences, the method,
  the results, the limits (the authors' and any the user noted), and related pages.
- The user's highlights, margin notes, questions, and disagreements are the core. Keep
  them in their own section, distinct from what the authors say.
- On concept pages, add what the paper contributes as that paper's finding, linked to its
  page: "Vaswani 외(2017)는 …을 보였다", "…라고 제안한다". Create a page for a method or
  term the paper introduces when it will be referred to again.
- A paper's result is evidence, not settled fact. Use "보고했다", "제안했다", "주장한다";
  keep every number with its dataset, sample, metric, and baseline; do not extend a result
  beyond the conditions tested. Say so when the source is a preprint.
- Summarize in your own words; do not reproduce the abstract or long passages. Write
  equations in TeX (`$…$`, `$$…$$`). Figures and tables you cannot see are described only
  as far as the text describes them.
- Assign the reference list, acknowledgements, author details, and publisher boilerplate
  to `null` with a reason. A cited work the user singled out is worth a mention or a link.
- Supplements suit a paper well: the prerequisites a reader lacks, the prior work it
  answers, and later results or criticism, each backed by a reference you read and dated.
  Search the wiki for other papers on the question and say how they agree or differ; when
  two sources conflict, record both with their attribution rather than choosing one.
- `category` is the field; tag with `논문` and the method or topic.
